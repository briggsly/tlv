## Executive Summary

The Latam to Tulsa program is actively onboarding LatAm-based founders into the Tulsa ecosystem. As of early 2026, the pipeline tracks **16 companies and 24+ individual founders** through a structured 6-stage funnel. **5 founders have clearly landed in Tulsa (3 pending arrival)** (from Monte Caldera, SphereBio, Qnity), **11 are in the visa pipeline** (InTulsa track), **5 are in the Deel visa pipeline**, and **2 companies are prospective** (pre-acceptance).

The funnel is operational — founders move from sourcing through application review, landing logistics, visa processing (concurrent with landing), community integration, and into ongoing support. The program has organized community events (Commerce Lunch & Learn, Founder Roundtables, Asado welcome event, Tulsa experiences) and maintains per-founder tracking via the Master Landing Tracker. However, several structural gaps weaken the funnel: corrupted source files obscure arrival history and contact data, buddy assignments are entirely empty, the event calendar goes largely TBD after May, and actual vs. target landing dates are inconsistently recorded.

**Key funnel health indicators:**

The funnel is strongest at the top (sourcing through landing) and thinnest at community integration and ongoing support, where structured data on outcomes is limited.

## Stage-by-Stage Narrative

### Stage 1: Sourcing

**Pipeline entry.** Founders enter the funnel through partner referrals, conferences, inbound inquiries, or outbound research. The 2024 GRIDX cohort (281 Asana tasks, Demo Day with 9 tasks) represents an earlier sourcing channel. Current pipeline companies include Monte Caldera (GridX), SphereBio (GridX), Qnity (GridX), NanoInGreen (GridX), Kresko (GridX), Kigui (<$1M), FeedVax (GridX), E-Pick (>$1M), Alkemio (>$1M), BioPlastix (GridX), Caligenia, Avatar, Avedian, MapVX, plus the 2024 historical cohort.

**Data quality:** Sourcing channel data exists in Landing Status Overview but is not structured as a standalone sourcing pipeline. The source-of-origin per founder is implicit (category assignment: GridX, <$1M, >$1M) rather than tracked as a sourcing channel field. No formal sourcing tracker was found — initial identification and fit-checking happens outside the visible tracking documents.

**Gaps:** No structured sourcing funnel (contacts in → applications out). Conversion from sourced-contact to applicant is not measurable with current data. Partner org relationships and referral sources are undocumented in the tracked data.

### Stage 2: Application

**Formal entry.** Founders submit applications, are reviewed and scored, and receive a decision. The Landing Status Overview captures Application Status and GKFF Approval Status per company. Book.xlsx (Founder Focused/Landing/ ARCHIVE sheet) is referenced as the application record system but contained only a template — no live application data was extractable.

**Current pipeline composition by category:**
- GridX companies: Monte Caldera, SphereBio, Qnity, NanoInGreen, Kresko, FeedVax, BioPlastix, Caligenia
- <$1M companies: Kigui, Avatar
- >$1M companies: E-Pick, Alkemio, Avedian, MapVX
- Historical: 2024 GRIDX cohort (Asana extracts)

**Data quality:** Application status is present (Approved / In Process / Not Yet Submitted) but application dates, review scores, and decision memos are not surfaced in the tracking documents. The application → approval conversion rate cannot be calculated without knowing the denominator of total applications received vs. those visible in the pipeline.

**Gaps:** No application review rubric or scoring data found. Approval decisions are tracked as binary (GKFF Approval Status) but decision rationale and timeline are absent. Book.xlsx's template-only state means the formal application record is either in a different location or maintained outside the OneDrive visible scope.

## Risks

### 1. Runway Cliff for InTulsa Pipeline Founders

Five companies in the visa pipeline have insufficient runway (<12 months) while visa processing continues:
- NanoInGreen (3 founders): 10 months runway — H-1B1/L-1 processing ongoing
- Kresko (Carla Borini): 6 months — RFE issued Jan 17
- Kigui (Mauricio Kremer): 6 months — L-1 petition drafting
- FeedVax (Luis Barletta): 7.5 months — L-1 petition drafting
- BioPlastix (Karina Campos): 11 months — profile enhancement on hold

If visa processing extends beyond these runway timelines, founders may run out of operating capital before or shortly after arrival, increasing the risk of startup failure post-relocation.

### 2. Data Fragmentation & Single Points of Failure

Critical arrival and expense data was stored in files that are now corrupted (LTT Arrival Spreadsheets 8-22 and 8-27, Expense Tracker). The program relies on individual xlsx files in nested OneDrive folders with no backup, no consolidated database, and no version history recovery path for corrupted files. A single corrupted file can erase an entire data category.

### 3. Buddy Program Non-Functionality

The Founder Buddy Program lists 12 founders but has 0 assignments. This is a soft infrastructure gap — founders arriving in Tulsa have no structured peer connection. In a program where community integration is the explicit goal of Stage 5, the buddy system is the primary structured mechanism for peer-to-peer connection, and it is non-operational.

### 4. Community Integration Has No Measurable Completion Criteria

The stage model flags that the minimum N for "events attended" as an exit criterion is undefined. Without a measurable definition of integration completion, all landed founders remain in Community Integration indefinitely, and the program cannot demonstrate throughput to Ongoing Support. Event attendance is tracked, but it is not gated against any threshold.

### 5. Ongoing Support Stage Is a "Black Box"

No tracking data exists for Stage 6. If the program intends to provide long-term portfolio support, fundraising assistance, or alumni programming, there is no infrastructure to track it. Founders who have been in Tulsa for 6+ months have no documented check-in cadence, no progress tracking, and no alumni status.

### 6. No Founder Churn Tracking

The pipeline shows zero founders lost — no rejections, no dropouts, no companies that went cold. This is almost certainly an artifact of incomplete tracking rather than a perfect retention rate. Without churn data, the program cannot diagnose where the funnel leaks.

### 7. Founder Count Discrepancies

The various tracking sources disagree on total founder counts:
- Landing Status Overview: 24+ founders by name
- LTT Company Arrival Summary 7-17: 14 founders in 2025, 23 by 2026
- Some founders (Fungilife, Qumir Nano) appear in Arrival Summary but not in Landing Status Overview

This discrepancy suggests the tracking views may not be synchronized, and the true pipeline size may differ from any single source.

## Open Questions

1. **What is the actual sourcing funnel?** How many founders are contacted per month, what is the application conversion rate, and which channels yield the best applicants? No sourcing tracker exists to answer this.

2. **Where is Book.xlsx's live data?** Book.xlsx exists as a template only. Is the application record maintained elsewhere (CRM, Airtable, another OneDrive location)? Without it, the application stage has no structured data.

3. **What happens when a founder's runway runs out mid-process?** Is there a contingency for founders whose operating capital depletes before or immediately after arrival? The data shows 8 founders with <12 months runway, but no contingency framework is documented.

4. **What defines "integrated" for Community Integration exit?** The stage model identifies this as needing a program policy decision. What is the minimum events-attended threshold, stakeholder introduction count, or survey score that defines integration completion?

5. **Is there a formal cohort model or are founders integrated individually?** The event calendar suggests both cohort events (Gradient Prototype to Pitch) and individual programming (Founder Check-Ins). The program's integration model should be explicit.

6. **How are founders on temporary status (tourist, B-1) while visa processes tracked?** Several founders may be in Tulsa on non-immigrant status while their formal visa (O-1, E-2, L-1) processes. This status is not tracked in current data and carries compliance risk.

7. **Does an alumni program exist, or is Ongoing Support the catch-all?** The stage model treats Ongoing Support as the terminal stage, but if the program intends an alumni track with different engagement, it should be defined.

8. **Why were 5+ files corrupted, and what recovery path exists?** The corruption of key files (arrival spreadsheets, contact info, expenses, conferences, Book.xlsx) represents a systemic data management risk. Is there a backup strategy going forward?

9. **What is the actual landed date for each founder labeled "Landed"?** The tracker marks founders as Landed but often lacks a specific arrival date. For cohort analysis, time-to-arrival and time-to-integration metrics require actual dates.

10. **Is the 2024 GRIDX cohort considered part of the same funnel or a separate program?** The 2024 GRIDX data (Asana extracts with 281 tasks, Demo Day) represents a prior cohort structure. Clarifying whether these founders are in the current funnel or a historical program affects pipeline sizing.

## Appendix: Stage Model Rules (from founder-funnel-stage-model.md v1.0)

1. **One stage per founder.** A founder is in exactly one primary stage at any given time. Visa processing (Stage 4) is concurrent with Landing or early Community Integration but does not change the primary stage.

2. **Stages are sequential but Visa runs parallel.** A founder moves Sourcing → Application → Landing → Community Integration → Ongoing Support. Visa processing begins at Landing and may extend beyond Community Integration start, but founder stage advances independently of visa status.

3. **Founder states ≠ event attendance.** Event attendance is a data field recorded per founder, not a stage transition mechanism. A founder in Application can attend community events; a founder in Ongoing Support can attend sourcing events. Events enrich the record but do not gate stage progression.

4. **Inactive / Lost is a state, not a stage.** Founders who decline, go cold, are rejected, or depart are moved to an inactive status within their last stage's record. They can re-enter at Sourcing if re-engaged.

5. **Conversion metrics are stage-to-stage.** Each stage measures conversion to the next stage. Overall funnel metrics (e.g., sourced → active-in-Tulsa) are derived by multiplying stage conversion rates.
